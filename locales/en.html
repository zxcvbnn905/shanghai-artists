About the Project

The Digital Mapping and Interactive Visualization Project of the Republican-Era Shanghai Art World is a decade-long interdisciplinary digital humanities initiative. Drawing on historical documents, spatial data, digital mapping, and field surveys of historic buildings, the project seeks to reconstruct the living spaces, social networks, and urban artistic ecology of artists active in Shanghai between 1912 and 1948.

The project originated in a research initiative funded in 2016 by the General Research Fund (GRF) of the Hong Kong Research Grants Council (RGC). Since then, the research team has collected extensive information on artists from a wide range of historical sources, including biographies, autobiographies, Republican-era newspapers and periodicals, scholarly books and articles, memoirs, and archival materials held by the Shanghai Municipal Archives. These materials have been cross-referenced with historical maps, city guides, and archival records to verify, locate, and digitize artists’ residential and activity addresses. In 2024, the project received additional research funding from Shanghai University, with faculty members and students joining its archival research, field surveys, website development, and design, providing important momentum for its continued development.

Over the years, the project has built a research database comprising 1,347 Chinese artists and 155 foreign artists, together with approximately 1,650 historical addresses. Using historical GIS, spatial statistics, spatial regression, and social network analysis, the research team has mapped artists’ residential distributions, clusters, migration trajectories, and social networks across different historical periods. These analyses examine the relationships between artistic communities and the spatial structures of the foreign concessions, art institutions, cultural facilities, and the broader urban social environment. Digital mapping thus serves not merely as a means of presenting historical information, but as a research tool for reinterpreting the art world of Republican-era Shanghai.

The project subsequently expanded from historical mapping to field investigations of artists’ former residences and surviving urban heritage. The team has conducted on-site surveys, photography, architectural typology identification, and historical verification of surviving residential sites associated with artists. This work reconnects addresses found in historical archives with the contemporary urban landscape of Shanghai and relates historical locations identified through mapping to surviving elements of the city’s cultural heritage.

Building on this long-term research, the project is now being developed into the “Digital Map and Interactive Platform of the Modern Shanghai Art World.” The website integrates historical maps, artists’ biographies, residential addresses, social relationships, art institutions, historic buildings, and related visual materials within a unified digital platform. Through interactive maps, temporal change, spatial search, and visual storytelling, the platform seeks to make scholarly research more accessible and useful for art history and urban history research, cultural heritage conservation, teaching, and public engagement.

The project has benefited from the participation and guidance of scholars and professionals specializing in historical GIS, cartography, art history, architecture, and digital technologies, including Atlas X. C. Guo (University of Wisconsin Cartography Lab), Sabrina P. Y. Zhang (GIS and Digital Humanities), and Zhen Fan (Art History), among others. It has gradually developed into an interdisciplinary collaboration spanning art history, urban studies, GIS, architecture, computer science, and visual design.

Selected Publications and Awards

The project has generated a series of scholarly publications on the artistic geography of Republican-era Shanghai, artists’ spatial clustering, the urban creative milieu, and artists’ social networks:

  1. Zheng, Jane, Sabrina P. Y. Zhang, Zhen Fan, Hui Lin, and Yuen-Sang Leung. 2024. “Rediscovering Shanghai Modern: Chinese Cosmopolitanism and the Urban Art Scene, 1912–1948.” Urban History 51(1): 198–232. DOI: 10.1017/S096392682200027X.
  2. Zheng, Jane, Jie He, Zhen Fan, Sabrina P. Y. Zhang, Hui Lin, and Yuen-Sang Leung. 2022. “Mapping the Most Influential Art Districts in Shanghai (1912–1948) through Clustering Analysis.” The Journal of Arts Management, Law, and Society 52(6): 345–368. DOI: 10.1080/10632921.2022.2080137.
  3. Zheng, Jane, Sabrina P. Y. Zhang, Zhen Fan, Hui Lin, Yuen-Sang Leung, and Yong Liu. 2024. “Modeling the Creative Milieu of Cosmopolitan Cities: Shanghai, 1912–1948.” The Journal of Arts Management, Law, and Society 54(1): 42–64. DOI: 10.1080/10632921.2023.2279072.
  4. Zheng, Jane, Sabrina P. Y. Zhang, Zhen Fan, Jie He, and Yuen-Sang Leung. 2025. “Spatial Proximity in Artists’ Social Networks: The Shanghai Art World, 1912–1948.” Transactions in Planning and Urban Research 4(2): 207–223. DOI: 10.1177/27541223241295624.

An interactive digital mapping project developed from this research received an Honorable Mention in the Digital Map category of the 2023 CaGIS Map Design Competition. The award-winning project has also provided an important foundation for the further development of the present digital mapping platform.

From an address discovered in an archive, to a coordinate on a historical map, and ultimately to a residence still standing in the streets of Shanghai today, this project seeks to reconnect artists, the city, and history. It not only documents the lives and movements of artists in Republican-era Shanghai, but also uses digital mapping to bring back into view an art world once deeply embedded in the everyday spaces of the city.
